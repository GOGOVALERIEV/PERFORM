The Top 10 Anti-AI Slop Skills For Better AI Writing

At this point, your customers can smell AI writing in one sentence, and every "it's not X, it's Y" tells them you didn't write it. AI dev Juampi Hernandez of Firecrawl ranked his top ten skills that kill those tells. Each are free. And you can drop them into any harness like Codex or Claude Codex to instantly make your AI sound less like AI.

💡 Key Insights:

  • Stop Slop grades your draft before you publish...it scores five traits, from directness to density, out of ten each, and a total under 35 sends it back for another pass.
  • Humanizer makes the rewrite sound like you...paste a few paragraphs of your writing and it copies your rhythm, word choice, and punctuation quirks, without inventing a fact to fill a gap.
  • Slopbeth catches the sentences that read clean but say nothing...it keeps every sourced fact, cuts unsourced claims, and flags the promises an edit changed, since a line made less true to fool a detector is worse than slop.

The New AI Divide: Who’s Using It and Where

WeWork's research team went through the Census Bureau's survey of American businesses to answer one plain question: nearly four years after ChatGPT arrived, how many companies use AI, and for what? The answer is one in five, mostly to write emails.

💡 Key Insights:

  • Anyone who builds a real AI workflow is already ahead of most adopters...only 21.7% of U.S. businesses used AI in July, 85.4% of them for writing and editing, and 64.3% changed nothing about how they operate.
  • Marketing is the door AI walks through first...Sales & Marketing leads every business function at 14.3%, and 62.8% of companies planning to adopt expect to start there.
  • Most holdouts think AI doesn't apply to them...61.6% of the businesses sitting it out say it isn't relevant to their work, and 22% admit they don't understand what it can do. That's the objection any AI pitch has to answer.

Prompt of the Week

This Week's Feature: 3 Astra Accelerators

GPT-6 Astra ships with its own instruction manual. The prompting guide OpenAI published alongside the model shows you the exact phrasing the model was trained to respond to, so you can get the top-tier version of Astra on day one.

We pulled three snippets that deliver the biggest upgrade. The first turns Astra into a finisher... hand it a task and it carries the whole thing through to done.

The second gives you cleaner, plain writing with less AI speak. The third makes sure your instructions always win, even when a skill or template says otherwise.

You can check out OpenAI's full Astra guidance here.

THE 3 FULL PROMPTS:

PROMPT #1 - Make GPT More Autonomous

"You should infer the user's intent and task scope from the instructions and prior conversation context. Your job is to bias towards action and carry the user's intended task to completion.

When the user expresses intent to perform new work or fix an existing issue, persist until the user's intended goal is complete. Progress autonomously towards the user's goal (e.g. creating isolated worktrees / checkouts if needed, resolving merge conflicts, read-only actions, creating draft PRs etc.) unless they are clearly destructive or irreversible."

PROMPT #2 - Eliminate AI Speak & Slop

"Avoid using slop words or phrases like "Bottom Line:" in conclusions, "delve," "foster," "leverage," "it's worth noting," "importantly," "Question? Answer." or "This isn't about X. It's about Y.", "genuinely" or hyphenated compound descriptions and adjectives. Do not use concluding summary statements such as "In short:..", "The simplest mental model is:...".

State the intended action directly. Avoid adding what you won't do, what will remain unchanged, or how you'll separate or categorize results. Do not use contrastive framing such as "X, not Y" or "X—not Y" that introduces an unprompted alternative that the user didn't ask about. Avoid invented compound labels like "exact-head checks" and "editorial-row layouts", vague qualifiers, and canned transitions; use plain verbs and prepositions to state the actual relationship directly."

PROMPT #3 - Better Instruction Following

"The user's instructions take precedence over guidelines provided in a skill. If explicit user instructions conflict with a skill's instructions, prioritize the user's instructions."
